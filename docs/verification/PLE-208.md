# PLE-208 — high-refresh timeline-shift release diagnosis

The two failed PLE-128 timeline-shift captures were caused by a presenter
release decision, not by a missing Perfetto atrace category.

## Evidence

Artifacts are under
`/home/wnt/gta6/build/dispatch/ple-128/captures/invalid/`.

* `dj_paced_shift_120.first-failed.json` records 884 codec inputs, 883 decoded
  outputs, zero render releases and an empty `queueBuffer_by_thread` map.
* The repeated capture's `dj_paced_shift_120_logcat.txt` enables balanced
  timestamped release at 120 Hz with `recovery=timeline_shift`. It then shows
  `dropped_presenter` rising from zero to 1,502 without an
  `AMediaCodec output release failed` message.
* A trace-processor query of the repeated
  `dj_paced_shift_120.perfetto-trace` finds two `queueBuffer` slices, both from
  `com.android.systemui`'s `RenderThread`, and none from
  `com.metallic.chiaki`. The `gfx` category was therefore present; Chiaki did
  not queue decoded frames to its surface.

## Cause and fix

`android_chiaki_video_recovery_action()` returned `DROP_HEAD` for the
`timeline_shift` strategy whenever the pacing mode was balanced. At 120 Hz,
once the 60 fps presentation timeline fell behind the next-vsync threshold,
`on_vsync()` repeatedly called `AMediaCodec_releaseOutputBuffer(..., false)`.
Those successful non-rendering releases explain both the rising presenter-drop
counter and the absence of Chiaki `queueBuffer` slices.

The decision now follows the configured strategy in every pacing mode:
`timeline_shift` shifts by whole vsync periods, while `flush` retains its
flush-and-reanchor behavior. The host unit test checks both strategies across
all pacing modes, including balanced.

The workspace `summarize.py` change distinguishes this signature from an empty
or otherwise invalid trace as `FAILED CAPTURE (decoded frames never queued to
surface)` when decoded slices exist but codec `queueBuffer` slices do not.
