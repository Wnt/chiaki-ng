# PLE-116 — A/B round 3: real PTS and PLE-23 at auto operating-rate 480; operating-rate sweep 480 / 960 / 1920 (2026-09-16, 06:33–06:55 UTC)

Follow-up of [`PTS-decode-latency.md`](PTS-decode-latency.md) (PLE-75) and [`PLE-79.md`](PLE-79.md).
PLE-75 found that the Exynos decoder's per-frame latency follows the frame rate it is told or infers, and
wired `operating-rate = 480` in automatically whenever PLE-16 real PTS or PLE-23 pacing is on. This round
asks the three questions PLE-75 left open (absorbing PLE-110, PLE-112, PLE-113): is real PTS / PLE-23 now
cost-free enough to promote, does the decoder level top out at 480, and does touch boost still add ~2 ms
on top of it. Sixteen scenarios, one build, one night, two baselines per arm.

**Headline:** the level does **not** top out at 480. `operating-rate = 960` takes decode latency from
7.6–8.0 ms to 6.8 ms mean (p95 9.6–9.8 → 8.4–8.6) untouched, and from 5.8–6.0 to 4.9 ms touched, with
*lower* decoder-thread CPU and no thermal step; 1920 adds nothing over 960. The gain does not depend on
real PTS: an explicit 960 on the default 1 µs-PTS path gets the same 6.8 ms. Real PTS and PLE-23 balanced at
the current auto 480 sit inside (or a hair above) the baseline band on every column: no measurable cost, no
measurable gain by themselves. Touch boost is still worth ~2 ms and is additive to the operating-rate level.

## What the numbers say about the mechanism

PLE-75's curve (decode latency against the frame rate the codec is told or infers) gets two more points and
one correction:

| rate seen by the codec | how | decode mean | p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | real PTS, no operating rate (PLE-75) | 13.9–14.2 | 28–33 |
| 240 | PLE-6 tier 0 before PLE-111 (PLE-75 exp 3); `stream_performance_mode` (PLE-79) | 9.4 / 7.9–8.0 | 11.6 / 9.8–9.9 |
| 480 | auto switch, PLE-111 tier 0 (this round, 3 runs) | 7.8–8.0 | 9.6–9.8 |
| default | 1 µs PTS steps, no operating rate (this round, 2 runs; PLE-75, PLE-79) | 7.6–8.2 | 9.6–10.3 |
| **960** | explicit, with or without real PTS (3 runs) | **6.8–6.9** | **8.4–8.6** |
| **1920** | explicit | **6.8** | **8.5** |

The correction: the default path's 1 µs timestamp steps do **not** buy the decoder's top level, as PLE-75
inferred from "1 MHz spacing". They buy exactly the 480 level (7.6–8.2 ms, same as the auto switch), and
`ResourceManagerService` reports the default path as `fps:30`, so the inferred rate is clamped or ignored
somewhere between the timestamps and the MFC QoS table. An explicit `operating-rate` goes past that clamp:
960 is one level higher (−1 ms mean, −1 ms p95, shorter tail: max 10.4–12.3 vs 13.9–21.4) and 1920 is the
same level again, so the table has one more useful step above 480 and none above 960 for this stream.

Touch boost is a different mechanism and stacks: at every operating rate, touching takes ≈1.9 ms off the
decode mean (default 7.6–8.0 → 5.8–6.0; 480: 7.8–8.0 → 5.9–6.1; 960: 6.8–6.9 → 4.9; 1920: 6.8 → 4.9). It comes
with the CPU clocks (little 865–918 vs 558–695 MHz, mid 1108–1390 vs 946–1318, big core unparked), i.e. the
CPU-side part of the input→output path, not the MFC level. So PLE-113's question has a plain answer: yes,
touch still adds ~2 ms on top of 480, and it still adds ~2 ms on top of 960.

## Verdicts

**(a) Should `stream_real_video_timestamps` and/or PLE-23 become default-on?**
At the current auto 480 they are **cost-free within the noise of this harness** (every latency column inside
or within 1 % of the n=2 band, untouched and touched; ~2 % more app CPU touched) and they **gain nothing
on these metrics** by themselves. The decode-latency reason to want them (round 2's 14 ms) is gone, and the
decode-latency reason to prefer them (the 960 level) turns out not to need them. So the promote decision is
about what PLE-23 is *for* (paced release, stale-frame dropping, the PLE-83/105 de-jitter work that
builds on real PTS), not about these columns. Recommendation: file the PLE-65-pattern promote ticket for
**PLE-23 balanced** (which implies real PTS) with this round's numbers as the "no cost" evidence, and let
the pacing-quality tickets (PLE-123's cadence counters, PLE-108) supply the "benefit" half; do not promote
real PTS alone, it has no benefit of its own. Not flipped here.

**(b) Which operating rate to bake into the auto switch?** **960.** Three 960 runs and two 1920 runs
agree to within 0.1 ms; 1920 adds nothing, 480 leaves ~1 ms mean and ~1 ms p95 on the table. And since the
gain is the operating rate's, not the timestamps', the switch's condition (real PTS on) is too narrow: the
same request on the default path (`rate960_nopts_untouched`) is the best untouched decode latency ever
measured on this phone (6.83 / 8.43). Two follow-ups filed: raise `DECODER_REAL_PTS_OPERATING_RATE` 480 →
960, and add a default-path operating-rate request behind a flag for its own A/B. Neither is done here.

**(c) Does touch boost still add ~2 ms on top of 480?** **Yes, ≈1.9 ms, and also on top of 960.** The two
effects are independent (MFC level vs CPU clocks), so the operating rate does not replace whatever a
"synthetic touch boost" (PLE-113's premise) would buy; that idea keeps its ~2 ms.

Consequence for `stream_performance_mode` (PLE-133, blocked on this ticket): its one live leg on Exynos,
`operating-rate = fps × 4 = 240`, is dominated by both the auto 480 and the 960 measured here; nothing in this
round argues for keeping the decoder leg.

## Harness lessons

- A worker editing `scripts/dev/ab/ab.sh` while another worker's round is running can kill a scenario
  (`n: unbound variable` for ~1 minute while PLE-134 landed). Freeze `ab.sh` + `chiaki_trace.cfg` into the
  run dir at the start of a round and point `chain.sh` at the copy (`AB_TRACE_CFG`, `AB_ADB`).
- `device-bin/adb` needs `ANDROID_SERIAL` whenever the emulator is up; `ab.sh` sets it, `mkprefs.py`,
  `install -r` and ad-hoc `dumpsys` do not.
- `Preferences.validateDecoderOperatingRate` silently clamped values above 1000 until this ticket; the
  on-device prefs file showed 1920 but the codec would have received 1000. Always check `algo.rate.value` in
  the logcat, not only `_prefs_on_device.xml`.
- The phone's persisted prefs are not the shipped defaults; a "restore byte-identical" round preserves
  whatever the previous round left (here `stream_performance_mode=true`). Pin every flag the baseline
  depends on in the baseline prefs file rather than trusting the base file.
