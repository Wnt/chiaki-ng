# PLE-356: what `videoPacketJitterMicros` actually measured

The field read **2.9 ms** while the phone's own ping to the console had a p10–p90 spread
of **25.4–57.6 ms**. `CONSTRAINED_JITTER_MS` and `POOR_JITTER_MS` are thresholds on that
number, so this is the same shape as the `q.rtt` defect [PLE-343](PLE-343.md) fixed: a
displayed quantity that does not move when the thing it names moves.

It was not a unit error and not a wrong threshold. The estimator was averaging the wrong
samples, and the size of the error depended on the bitrate.

Device: Galaxy S22 Ultra, SM-S908B, `192.168.40.101:5555`, on SSID `Kari-Impair`
(VLAN 40) behind the PLE-349 impairment rig. Console: PS5-466, `192.168.1.164`.

## Ground truth

The phone's own `ping -D -i 1 192.168.1.164`, which crosses the shaped path. ADB is
exempt from the shaping, so ADB round-trip time is never used here.

RFC 3550's jitter is an EWMA of |D|, the difference between consecutive
*inter-arrival* and *inter-departure* times, so the directly comparable ground truth is
the ping's **mean |consecutive difference|**, not its spread. Both are reported below;
the spread is what the ticket quoted, the mean |Δ| is what the metric estimates.

## 1. The measurement: the field does not track the path

Re-analysed from [PLE-343](PLE-343.md)'s own capture — one continuous stream with the
impairment profile stepped underneath it every 60 s, first 10 s of each phase dropped
while the shaper step settles. `docs/verification/PLE-356/analyze-baseline.py`, output
in `baseline.txt`, against `build/captures/ple343-verify/session_logcat.txt`.

| phase | ping med | ping p10–p90 | **ping mean \|Δ\|** | probe RTT | **`packet_jitter_ms`** | pkts/s | fps | pkts/frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 5.0 | 3.7–7.9 | 1.8 | 5.2 | 2.14 | 680 | 59.9 | 11.3 |
| 5g | 25.8 | 20.4–30.6 | 3.9 | 25.8 | 3.11 | 686 | 59.9 | 11.4 |
| 4g | 61.9 | 52.6–69.9 | 7.7 | 63.0 | 2.72 | 608 | 59.9 | 10.2 |
| wifi-slow | 37.0 | 25.4–57.6 | **13.3** | 40.7 | **2.89** | 569 | 59.9 | 9.5 |
| clean | 4.7 | 3.6–8.9 | 2.5 | 5.5 | 2.53 | 560 | 59.9 | 9.3 |

The real delay variation moves **1.8 → 13.3 ms**, a factor of 7.4. The field moves
**2.14 → 3.11 ms**, a factor of 1.45, and not monotonically: the 4G phase, with twice
the clean delay variation of 5g, reads *lower* than 5g. It never reaches
`CONSTRAINED_JITTER_MS = 4.0`, let alone `POOR_JITTER_MS = 10.0`, on any profile the rig
can produce. **The jitter arm of the badge was unreachable in practice**, and the number
beside it in the overlay was not a measurement of the path.

## 2. What it was measuring instead

`chiaki_takion_video_packet_jitter_push` (`lib/src/takion.c`) ran RFC 3550 A.8 over
*every video packet*, taking each packet's nominal send time from `frame_index / fps`.
PLE-202 added it as "true Takion video interarrival jitter", and interarrival jitter is
what it computed — but a video frame is carried by many packets that all share one
`frame_index`. For those packets the sender delta is exactly zero, so the sample becomes
the intra-burst gap: tens of microseconds, a property of the NIC and the sender's pacing,
not of the path.

The capture above measured **9.3–11.4 packets per frame**, so roughly nine samples in ten
were intra-burst gaps and one in ten was the frame boundary that carried the real delay
variation. A 1/16-gain EWMA over that mixture converges near the mean of all of them.

`test/takion.c` asserted this as intended behaviour: pushing a second packet of the same
frame 1000 µs after the first *lowered* the estimate from 1938 to 1879.

### Why no threshold change could have fixed it

`docs/verification/PLE-356/dilution.py` runs both estimators over a synthetic stream —
each frame alternately early and late by a known amount, delivered as N packets. Output
in `dilution.txt`:

| real PDV | pkts/frame | per-frame (fix) | per-packet (old) | old/real |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 1 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.000 |
| 1.0 | 10 | 1.00 | 0.12 | 0.117 |
| 10.0 | 10 | 10.00 | 0.78 | 0.078 |
| 32.0 | 5 | 32.00 | 5.64 | 0.176 |
| **32.0** | **10** | **32.00** | **2.40** | **0.075** |
| 32.0 | 20 | 32.00 | 0.85 | 0.027 |

At the measured ~10 packets per frame, a real 32 ms of delay variation reports as
**2.40 ms** — the ticket observed 2.9 ms on a path with ~32 ms of spread, so the model
reproduces the reading. The attenuation ratio ranges from 1.000 to 0.027 across the
table and depends on both the packets per frame *and* the size of the variation, because
the intra-burst floor is additive. It is not a constant, so no rescaling and no
re-chosen threshold could have recovered the real number. **Scaling it would have
produced a plausible-looking lie**, exactly as [PLE-343](PLE-343.md) warned.

A second consequence of the same floor: `CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_GOOD_JITTER_US` is
2000, and the clean-LAN reading is 2.14–2.53 ms. The adaptive loss report's jitter
recovery arm could not assert on an idle LAN.

## 3. Whether packet interarrival jitter is the right input at all

It is, once it is measured per frame — but it is not interchangeable with path delay
variation, and the difference matters for the badge.

- What hurts the viewer is **when a frame is ready to present**, not when its 47th packet
  arrived. A frame is not decodable until its last packet lands, and the presenter's
  de-jitter buffer is sized in frame periods. A per-frame delay variation is therefore
  closer to the experience than a per-packet one, and strictly closer than the ping.
- It is **one-way and downstream**, where the ping is a round trip. Under a symmetric
  netem profile the per-frame figure should read roughly the downstream share of the
  ping's mean |Δ|, so the two should track but not coincide. Reported as a ratio, not
  asserted as equality.
- It is **not** a substitute for the RTT arm, which PLE-343 already put on the
  heartbeat→DATA_ACK probe. Steady delay and delay variation are different complaints
  and the badge needs both; the 4G row above is the case that separates them — 62 ms of
  delay with only 7.7 ms of variation.

So the jitter arm stays, with a measurement behind it instead of a placeholder.

## 4. The fix

One sample per frame, taken from the first packet received for each frame index, against
the nominal `frame_delta / fps` cadence:

- further packets of the frame already being measured contribute nothing, because their
  nominal send time is identical and they carry no new information about the path;
- lost frames are priced against the cadence via `frame_delta`, so three missing frames
  arriving on schedule are not jitter;
- a frame index *older* than the current one is a reordering artifact and is dropped
  without disturbing the state, so the next real frame is still priced correctly;
- only a gap above `CHIAKI_TAKION_VIDEO_JITTER_MAX_FRAME_DELTA` (120 frames, two seconds
  at 60 fps) resyncs silently. A `blip-200ms` stall is twelve frames at 60 fps: that is
  real delay variation and it counts.

The superseded per-packet EWMA is kept and logged as `packet_jitter_raw_ms` beside the
new value, wired to nothing, so a single capture shows both — the same treatment
[PLE-343](PLE-343.md) gave `console_rtt_raw`.

**This is a defect fix, not a behaviour change: it ships on and unconditional, with no
flag** (AGENTS rule 4). The thresholds are untouched. They were chosen for a quantity in
milliseconds of delay variation, and that is now what they receive; the diagnosis is that
the input was wrong, not the boundaries — the same conclusion PLE-343 reached about
`POOR_RTT_MS`.

## 5. Unit tests

`test/takion.c`, chained from `test_network_stats_all()` via
`test_takion_format_congestion`, so they do not appear in `chiaki-unit --list`. Verified
to run by breaking one assertion and watching the count drop **124 → 123**, then restored
to 124.

They cover: the stable-cadence and frame-index-wrap case; that further packets of a frame
do not move the estimate *while the superseded estimator provably does*; that a frame
delivered as eleven packets gives the same answer as the same frame delivered as one;
lost frames priced against the cadence and a reordered older frame ignored; a
`blip-200ms`-sized stall registering as 12.5 ms while a 121-frame index discontinuity
resyncs silently; a zero `fps` recording nothing; and the whole defect end to end —
10 ms of per-frame delay variation over a ten-packet-per-frame stream converging to
9.5–10.5 ms on the fix while the per-packet form lands below a quarter of it.

## Not proven on the console

The after-capture against the fixed build did not run. The PS5 refused to start video
from the first retry onwards —

```
Session quit: reason=remote_disconnected
  remote_reason="Nagare did not init! AvCap failed to initialize video: [InitResult:-6]"
```

— after the first capture attempt was aborted mid-stream by an unrelated `impairctl`
failure (see below) and the app was force-stopped without a clean Disconnect. It did not
clear in ~35 minutes across seven attempts, survived a wake packet, and reproduced on
both the Low latency and Data saver quality presets, so it is not profile-specific.
Discovery answers `HTTP/1.1 200 Ok` with no running title throughout, and the phone
reaches Takion and Ctrl every time; it is the console's AV capture that is wedged. There
is no programmatic standby: the fork app's console menu offers only Edit and Delete, and
`chiaki-cli` has `discover` and `wakeup` only. **Clearing it needs someone at the
console.**

What that leaves unverified on hardware: the absolute value the fixed metric reads under
each profile, its ratio to the ping's mean |Δ|, and the badge's resulting jitter verdict.
The harness to produce it is committed and ready — `docs/verification/PLE-356/capture.sh`
(profiles `clean 5g wifi-slow clean blip-200ms clean`, 60 s each, under one continuous
stream) and `analyze-capture.py`, which joins `packet_jitter_ms`, `packet_jitter_raw_ms`,
the ping's median, p10–p90 and mean |Δ|, the probe RTT and the badge's jitter verdict per
phase. It is one run away once the console is healthy.

## Two rig notes this run paid for

**`blip-200ms` cannot be entered from `wifi-slow`.** The switch leaves the previous
profile's `20mbit` rate cap in force, and verify then reports
`rate: 20mbit in force, expected no rate cap`. Entered from `clean` it applies correctly.

**`blip-200ms` always fails verify.** Its steady state is `delay 0ms`, which `tc` omits
from its own status output, so `verify_switch` reports `delay: missing, expected 0ms`
forever — the same class of false negative as [PLE-354](https://linear.app/pleikkari/issue/PLE-354).
The profile is genuinely in force: a phone ping under it reads a ~14 ms baseline with
206/207/409 ms spikes. `capture.sh` passes `--no-verify` for that profile only, with the
reason inline.

**A capture script must wait for the stats line, not for `StreamActivity`.** One run
stepped all six profiles over seven minutes under a session the console had killed two
seconds in: `StreamActivity` was topmost the whole time, showing an error. `capture.sh`
now waits for three `Feedback stats:` lines and aborts on `Session has quit`, which turned
the next failure into a 20-second one.
