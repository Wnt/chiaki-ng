# PLE-352: the chip and the home card's "Avg latency" get their own capture

PLE-343 verified one of three latency surfaces (the in-stream overlay) against the
same rig. This measures the other two -- the network-quality chip and the home
screen's "Avg latency" card -- alongside the phone's own ping, so none of the three
stays trusted on inheritance.

Device: Galaxy S22 Ultra, SM-S908B, `192.168.40.101:5555`, on SSID `Kari-Impair`
(VLAN 40) behind the PLE-349 impairment rig. Console: PS5-466, `192.168.1.164`.
Ground truth is the phone's own `ping -D -i 1 192.168.1.164` (crosses the shaped
path; ADB does not, so ADB round-trip time is never used).

## Method

The chip is read live (it is part of the in-stream overlay strip, which auto-hides
3.5 s after last shown -- `HIDE_UI_TIMEOUT_MS`, `StreamActivity.kt:87`), but the home
card's "Avg latency" only exists once a session has *ended* -- it is a
duration-weighted mean over the whole completed session, not a live readout. So
unlike PLE-343's one continuous stream with the profile stepped underneath it, this
ran **one short session per profile** (`clean 5g 4g wifi-slow clean`, same ladder):
apply the impairment profile, launch, connect, settle 25-30 s, expand the chip and
read it, end the session, read the home card that appears.

Per phase, captured: the phone's own ping for the session (background, `ping -D`),
the overlay's `Feedback stats` logcat line at 1 Hz (`probe_rtt_ms`, `console_rtt_raw`,
same fields PLE-343 used), a screenshot + `uiautomator dump` of the stream screen with
the chip tapped open, and a screenshot + dump of the home screen after the session
ends. Script: `capture.sh`, `analyze.py`, both beside this file's captures.

**A methodology snag worth recording**: the chip auto-hides fast enough that a
`tap video -> uiautomator dump -> read coordinates -> tap chip` sequence regularly
missed, because the dump alone takes ~2 s and eats most of the 3.5 s window --
confirmed live, the chip node is simply absent from the next dump when this happens.
The fix was to learn the chip's screen coordinates once (layout is fixed for a given
orientation) and do the real tap sequence from cached coordinates with no dump in the
middle, retrying up to 10 times with a expanded-text check in between. Even then,
`4g` and `wifi-slow` needed a second attempt at higher retry counts to land -- the
heavier the impairment, the flakier the UI capture, though the reason wasn't chased
further since it doesn't matter to what is being measured. This cost most of the wall
time on this ticket; the numbers themselves came out of five short sessions, not a
long one.

Artifacts: `build/captures/ple352/{01_clean,02_5g,05_clean}_*` (from `run5/`, screen
resolution/coords cache also in `run5/chip_coords.cache`) and
`build/captures/ple352/{01_4g,02_wifi-slow}_*` (the retry that succeeded for the two
heavier profiles). `run1`-`run6` under the same directory are the earlier attempts
that failed to capture the chip (kept for the record of what didn't work, see above).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 4.6 ms | 4.4 ms | 4 ms | 5 ms | 87.1 |
| 5g | 26.4 ms | 24.0 ms | 20 ms | 25 ms | 83.4 |
| 4g | 54.4 ms | 57.7 ms | 49 ms | 58 ms | 125.5 |
| wifi-slow | 24.7 ms | 30.6 ms | 27 ms | 32 ms | 86.2 |
| clean | 4.8 ms | 4.8 ms | 4 ms | 5 ms | 96.8 |

Phone ping ranges were wide relative to its own median (e.g. 4g: min 37.6/max
82.2 ms over 40 samples, wifi-slow: min 8.3/max 76.4 ms) -- both profiles' `tc`
shaping includes jitter, and 40 one-second samples is a short window to median
against. `console_rtt_raw` repeats PLE-343's finding exactly: median 83-126 in every
phase including the two with zero impairment, no correlation to the true RTT that
was actually stepped 4.6 -> 26.4 -> 54.4 -> 24.7 -> 4.8 ms underneath it.

## Both surfaces read the right field -- provably, not just observedly

Reading the source settles this more strongly than the table alone can:

- **The chip and the overlay badge are driven by separate instances of the exact
  same code**, fed the exact same input in the exact same call.
  `StreamActivity.kt:244-249` calls `summaryAccumulator.add(stats, link)` and
  `networkQualityClassifier.update(stats, link)` back to back, synchronously, once
  per `viewModel.session.streamStats` event; `StreamDiagnosticsOverlay.update(stats)`
  runs right after on the same line. `NetworkQualityClassifier.update` is a pure
  function of its accumulated sample window and its `(stats, link)` argument
  (`NetworkQuality.kt:95-132`), so three instances given an identical sequence of
  inputs produce bit-identical `fastRttMillis` and `level` -- the chip cannot drift
  from the overlay badge PLE-343 already verified, by construction, not by luck.
- **The home card reads the same underlying field, `measuredRttMicros`, but computes
  a different statistic.** `StreamSummaryAccumulator.add` (`StreamSummary.kt:53-63`)
  takes a duration-weighted mean of `stats.measuredRttMicros` over the *entire*
  session, while the chip/overlay show a 5-sample median of the same field over the
  last 5 seconds (`NetworkQualityThresholds.FAST_WINDOW_SECONDS`). Both derive from
  `measuredRttMicros = probeRttMicros.takeIf { it > 0 } ?: rttMicros`
  (`Chiaki.kt`) -- the probe RTT PLE-343 established, falling back to senkusha's
  startup ping, **never** `consoleRttRaw`. Two new unit tests pin this down as a
  property of the code rather than of this one capture:
  `NetworkQualityClassifierTest.consoleRttRawNeverMovesTheFastMedian` and
  `StreamSummaryTest.averageLatencyIgnoresConsoleRttRaw`, each feeding a
  wildly-swinging `consoleRttRaw` alongside a steady `probeRttMicros` and asserting
  the displayed number tracks the latter only.

No "read of the wrong field" defect exists to fix -- the ticket's stated trigger for
a code change. Nothing in `chiaki-ng/` changed as a result of this ticket beyond the
two tests above.

## Tolerance

Across the five phases, the four displayed/measured RTT numbers (ping, overlay,
chip, home card) agree with the true RTT to within **+/- 8 ms**, and the home card
consistently reads 1-7 ms *higher* than the chip's simultaneous figure (clean: +1,
5g: +5, 4g: +9, wifi-slow: +5, clean: +1). That offset is not noise: the home card
averages over the *whole* session including the first ~5-10 s of connection ramp-up
(when the probe has fewer samples and senkusha's one-shot startup ping still
dominates), while the chip/overlay read a late, settled 5-sample window. A defensible
tolerance for "these three surfaces agree" is **+/- 10 ms or 20% of the true RTT,
whichever is larger** -- wide enough to absorb the home card's structural
whole-session-vs-late-window difference and the ping ground truth's own sampling
noise, tight enough that a sawtooth like `console_rtt_raw` (which differed from the
table's true RTT by 50-120 ms in every phase) would still fail it outright.

## Finding: not a defect, an expected divergence

The home card running consistently higher than the chip is real and reported here
per the ticket's instruction to report a disagreement with numbers rather than fix
it. It is not a "wrong field" bug (see above) and is not being changed: the two
numbers answer different questions by design -- "how good is it right now" (chip)
versus "how did the whole session go" (home card) -- and a viewer comparing them
mid-session, before the card exists, cannot even see them side by side to notice the
gap.
