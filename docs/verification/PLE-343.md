# PLE-343: what `ConnectionQualityPayload.rtt` actually is

The overlay read **Poor / ~100 ms** while the phone was 3 ms from its PS5. This is the
measurement that settled what the number was, and the two defects it exposed in the
round trip that replaced it.

Device: Galaxy S22 Ultra, SM-S908B, `192.168.40.101:5555`, on SSID `Kari-Impair`
(VLAN 40) behind the PLE-349 impairment rig. Console: PS5-466, `192.168.1.164`.
Ground truth is the phone's own `ping -D -i 1 192.168.1.164`, which crosses the shaped
path; ADB does not, so ADB round-trip time is never used here.

Method: **one continuous stream**, with the impairment profile stepped underneath it
every 60 s from CT950 — no reassociation, no phone interaction, so senkusha's startup
measurement and the session state are identical across all five phases. `q.rtt` comes
from the `ConsoleRtt raw=` line (one per console message) and the rest from the 1 Hz
`Feedback stats` line, both behind "Log stream statistics (diagnostic)".

Captures: `build/captures/ple343` (before) and `build/captures/ple343-verify` (after),
with `capture.sh`, `analyze.py` and `replay.py` beside them.

## 1. `q.rtt` is a sawtooth, not a round trip

The real round trip was stepped **5.0 → 25.8 → 61.9 → 37.0 → 4.7 ms** under an
unbroken session. `q.rtt`'s median across those five phases was **110.9, 113.5, 98.2,
106.9, 100.7** — flat, and its range was the same 0–207 in every one. It does not move
when the thing it is named after moves by a factor of twelve.

What it does instead, over 322 console messages arriving at a median 1.013 s apart:

- resets to a peak (median 186.6, range 134.7–207.6),
- ramps **down** monotonically at a median −27.1 units/s,
- to a trough (median 17.0, min 0.000), then resets and repeats;
- 47 teeth in 350 s, period median 6.6 s; 84 % of consecutive samples are ≤ their
  predecessor; global range 0.000–207.561, mean 105.2.

The user's "98 ms Avg latency" is that mean. Averaging a ramp from ~200 to ~0 gives
~100 whatever the network is doing, which is why the figure looked stable and wrong at
the same time. A field that reads ~100 on a 3 ms LAN and ~100 on a 62 ms 4G link is not
a measurement of anything we can label, so it drives nothing: it is carried as
`console_rtt_raw`, shown in the overlay as `console-rtt N (unverified)`, and logged.

The earlier PLE-246 reading of "193 → 68 ms decaying over 8 s" was one tooth of this
sawtooth seen without enough samples to see it repeat.

## 2. The probe is a round trip, and agrees with ping

The heartbeat→DATA_ACK probe tracked the phone's own ping through every profile:
5.2/5.0, 25.8/25.8, 63.0/61.9, 40.7/37.0, 5.5/4.7 ms. Same UDP socket and 5-tuple as
the video, no extra packets. `senkusha` is a real measurement too but is taken once at
session start: it read 6.32 ms for all five phases, including the 4G one.

## 3. Two defects the calibration run exposed

**A single retransmitted heartbeat read as a 228.8 ms round trip.** At t=111 s of the
first run, on the 5g profile, one probe sample read **228.80 ms** while ping read
24.8 ms. `Takion Send Buffer re-sending packet with seqnum 0x87218935` is logged 0.7 s
earlier: the heartbeat was lost, resent after the 200 ms timer, and the ack that
arrived could not be attributed to either transmission. This is exactly the case
**Karn's algorithm** exists for, and the sample is now discarded — counted as
`probe_rtt_ambiguous` so the loss it signals stays visible, while the loss counters
that already drive the badge in their own right are untouched. The second run hit one
retransmit, recorded it as ambiguous, and its worst probe sample was 80.5 ms.

**A five-sample mean let that one outlier decide the verdict.** 228.8 ms in a
five-sample mean of a 25 ms link gives 65 ms, which is Poor — and POOR then needs its
30 s slow window under 30 ms to release, so the badge read Poor for **190 s** of a link
that was fine. The window aggregate is now the median, which is 25 ms for the same
five samples. Anything lasting long enough for a viewer to notice moves the median too.

## 4. The thresholds were never the problem

`POOR_RTT_MS = 40`, `CONSTRAINED_RTT_MS = 20`, `GOOD_RTT_MS = 15` map the measured
table onto exactly the verdicts the conditions deserve — 5 ms GOOD, 26 ms CONSTRAINED,
41 and 63 ms POOR — so they are unchanged. The diagnosis was that a placeholder and an
outlier were being fed through them, not that the boundaries were in the wrong place.

## Known limit

Leaving a genuinely bad profile, the badge takes ~25 s to return to GOOD: the 30 s slow
window is combined with `max()`, so it keeps the worst of the last 30 s. That is the
intended anti-flap behaviour and it does converge, but a shorter memory on the way down
is worth considering separately.
