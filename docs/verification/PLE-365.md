# PLE-365: re-deriving the adaptive loss report's jitter bounds

`CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_GOOD_JITTER_US = 2000` and
`CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_POOR_JITTER_US = 5000` (`lib/include/chiaki/congestioncontrol.h`)
were chosen against the old per-packet jitter estimator's clean-LAN floor of 2.14-2.53 ms
([PLE-356](PLE-356.md)). That estimator measured intra-burst packet gaps, not the path, so
neither bound was ever checked against a real measurement of the thing it gates. This is
analysis over the captures PLE-356 and PLE-357 already took (`build/captures/ple356`,
`build/captures/ple357`); no new device run.

## What input these bounds actually see

`chiaki_adaptive_loss_report_update` (`lib/src/congestioncontrol.c`) is fed
`chiaki_takion_get_video_packet_jitter_us(control->takion)` once per second
(`CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_SAMPLE_INTERVALS = 5` times the 200 ms congestion-control
tick). That is the **instantaneous per-frame EWMA reading PLE-356 fixed, sampled once a
second** -- not the badge's `NetworkQuality.kt` windowed median. The two consumers read the
same underlying metric at different resolutions, so the badge's own thresholds
(`CONSTRAINED_JITTER_MS = 4.0`, `POOR_JITTER_MS = 10.0`, unchanged and out of scope here)
are not directly reusable: a windowed median is far less noisy than a single 1 Hz sample of
an EWMA. The bounds here had to be re-derived against the noisier signal they actually gate.

## The measured distribution

Both captures log `packet_jitter_ms` once per second throughout. Re-parsed here
(`analyze.py`'s own per-phase table already exists in each capture; this pass extracts the
raw per-second samples instead of the phase median, dropping the first 10 s of each phase
for shaper settling, the same convention PLE-356's `analyze-capture.py` uses):

| phase | n | p50 | p90 | p99 | max | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clean (6 phases, both captures) | 335 | 1.98 | 2.50 | 2.90 | **3.70** | ple356 01/04/06, ple357 01/03/05 |
| 5g | 55 | 2.49 | 3.01 | 3.58 | 3.69 | ple356 02 |
| 4g | 57 | 2.60 | 3.74 | 4.62 | **4.90** | ple357 02 |
| wifi-slow | 56 | 7.43 | 8.54 | 9.66 | 9.84 | ple356 03 |
| blip-200ms | 108 | 1.63-2.30 | 2.14-2.80 | 7.21-43.80 | 9.87 / 89.80 | ple356 05, ple357 04 |

5g and 4g are the phases PLE-356's after-capture verdict table classified GOOD against the
badge's own rule (median well under `CONSTRAINED_JITTER_MS`), so they are the ceiling of
"still a good network," not an impairment this gate should ever call poor. wifi-slow is the
one phase PLE-356 classified CONSTRAINED; it is the floor of "genuinely degraded."

## Why 2000/5000 were wrong, and what replaces them

**`GOOD_JITTER_US = 2000`** sat *below the clean-LAN per-second median* (1.98 ms combined,
and one of the two capture sessions ran a clean-phase median of 2.1-2.4 ms on its own). The
recovery arm needs `CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_RECOVER_SAMPLES = 30` **consecutive** samples
at or under the bound. At a threshold sitting at the noise floor's own median, roughly half
of truly clean samples individually fail it, so a run of 30 is not merely slow -- it is
statistically unreachable within any session length this rig has run. This reproduces, with
a real distribution behind it, the same defect PLE-356 diagnosed against the old estimator's
floor: the recovery arm could not assert on an idle LAN.

**`POOR_JITTER_US = 5000`** was directionally fine -- it sits above 4g/5g and below
wifi-slow -- but the margin above the observed 4g ceiling (4.90 ms) was **100 microseconds**.
Two capture sessions of the same nominal `clean`/`5g`/`4g` profiles already show session-to-
session baseline drift of half a millisecond (clean medians of 1.53-1.63 ms in one session,
2.10-2.40 ms in the other, per PLE-356's and this ticket's own tables). A bound with 100 us
of headroom does not survive that drift; the next 4g run could just as easily read 5.0+ and
trip poor on a network the badge itself would call good.

Re-derived to sit outside the full observed range of the class of path each must not fire on,
rather than at the edge of one capture:

- **`GOOD_JITTER_US = 4000`** (4 ms): clears the combined clean max (3.70 ms) and the 5g/4g
  max (4.90 ms is still above it, which is intentional -- see below) with margin, so a truly
  clean run reaches 30 consecutive good samples with near certainty rather than never.
- **`POOR_JITTER_US = 6000`** (6 ms): keeps wifi-slow's near-total exceedance (89% of its
  samples, no run of 3 consecutive samples under it in either capture) while giving 1.1 ms
  (22%) of headroom over the observed 4g ceiling instead of 100 us.

Sample classification at the new bounds, same phases as above (`<=4000us` counts toward
recovery, `>=6000us` counts toward capping, the gap in between is deliberately neutral and
resets both counters -- this is the "separate enter/exit gates" hysteresis the header already
described, just retuned against measured data):

| phase | n | <=4000us (good) | >=6000us (poor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean | 335 | 100% | 0% |
| 5g | 55 | 100% | 0% |
| 4g | 57 | 91% | 0% |
| wifi-slow | 56 | 0% | 89% |
| blip-200ms | 108 | 97% | 2% |

4g's remaining 9% (samples between 4000 and 4900 us) land in the neutral dead zone, which
only resets the consecutive-good counter -- it does not push the state toward poor. That is
the intended behaviour: 4g is a good network by the badge's own classification, so it should
delay recovery confirmation, not trigger it, and it must never trigger poor.

## What each bound now means in terms of the path

Using the ratio PLE-356 established between this per-frame jitter estimator and the phone's
own round-trip ping mean |consecutive difference| (0.64-1.09 across clean/5g/wifi-slow/clean,
§6 of PLE-356.md):

- **4000 us (GOOD)** corresponds to roughly 4-6 ms of round-trip delay variation -- above the
  1.4-2.5 ms clean-LAN band and the 3.9 ms 5g band this rig produced, comfortably below the
  next phase up.
- **6000 us (POOR)** corresponds to roughly 6-9 ms of round-trip delay variation -- inside the
  10.8 ms wifi-slow band and clearly above the 5g/4g band, so it fires on a path exhibiting
  real, sustained delay variation rather than reacting to decoder-side EWMA noise.

## blip-200ms: not moved, and not claimed to be fixed here

The 200 ms stall covers about 1% of the wall clock. At 1 Hz sampling, it usually surfaces as
a single elevated sample (2% of blip-200ms samples land at or above the new 6000us POOR
bound in this data, one capture's single spike reaching 9.87 ms and the other's 89.80 ms).
`CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_ENTER_SAMPLES = 3` requires three **consecutive** poor samples,
and a single-second spike from a 200 ms event essentially never produces three in a row. So
this gate still cannot see a recurring `blip-200ms`-shaped stall, same as the badge PLE-356
and [PLE-366](https://linear.app/pleikkari/issue/PLE-366) already documented for the median
window. That is [PLE-366](https://linear.app/pleikkari/issue/PLE-366)'s territory (a
count-above-threshold arm, not a threshold move) and is not addressed by this ticket.

## Unit tests

`test/networkstats.c`, `test_adaptive_loss_report_jitter_boundaries_from_capture`, chained
from `test_network_stats_all()` alongside the existing adaptive-loss-report tests -- it does
not appear in `chiaki-unit --list` (verified: `--list` shows 60 entries, none matching
`networkstats`). Uses representative real sample values from the captures above (clean at
1.53/1.99/2.40/2.90/3.70 ms, the 4g ceiling at 4.90 ms, wifi-slow's median at 7.43 ms) rather
than round synthetic numbers, so a regression that drifts a bound back toward 2000/5000 us
would misclassify real recorded data, not just a contrived edge case.

Verified to execute: broke the final assertion
(`CHIAKI_ADAPTIVE_LOSS_REPORT_REASON_JITTER` -> `_REASON_GOOD`) and watched
`chiaki-unit`'s count drop from `124 of 124` to `123 of 124`, then restored it.

## This is a defect fix

Both bounds were chosen against a number (the old per-packet estimator's floor) that never
tracked the network; `GOOD_JITTER_US` in particular made the recovery arm unreachable in
practice, on any network. Per AGENTS rule 4 this ships on and unconditional -- there is
nothing to A/B when the old bound was calibrated against a value that no longer exists in the
code (PLE-356 removed the per-packet estimator from the state this arm reads back in
`chiaki_takion_get_video_packet_jitter_us`; only `packet_jitter_raw_ms`, wired to nothing,
remains for diagnostics).

## What is still not proven

- No device run was taken for this ticket (by design, per the brief); the derivation is
  entirely a re-analysis of PLE-356/PLE-357's existing captures. A future capture at the new
  bounds would confirm the adaptive loss report's `uncapped`/`capped` transitions on-console,
  which no capture so far has logged (PLE-356 and PLE-357 both logged the classifier/badge
  path, not `chiaki_adaptive_loss_report_update`'s own transition log line).
- The two captures are both from the same rig (`Kari-Impair`, VLAN 40) and phone
  (`SM-S908B`); the half-millisecond session-to-session baseline drift observed between them
  is the only evidence of drift this analysis has, not a characterization of drift in general.
- `blip-200ms` remains invisible to this arm, as documented above; not claimed fixed.
